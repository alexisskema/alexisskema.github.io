The cut-offs

In an ultra, a cut-off is a checkpoint where you can be pulled from the race. Recruiters tend to spot five in my profile. Here's how I'd answer them.

Pick a cut-off to see my answer.

Cut-off 1Only if you go by job titles. Almost three years in headhunting and consulting gave me rigour, technical depth and the habit of hitting deadlines. They also showed me I'm at my most effective with clients: talking things through, winning people over, seeing a project move because of a conversation. So I'd rather start on the bottom rung of sales and learn the job properly.

Cut-off 2True, and I won't pretend otherwise. What I do have is the view from the other side of the table: I chose an AI platform, worked with the vendor and got around 200 people using it. I know what makes a buyer hesitate and what gets a team to adopt a tool. Selling is what I want to learn, and I pick things up fast, mostly from the people around me.

Cut-off 3Not officially. In executive search, each placement was billed at 20% of the salary, so my 13 placements add up to over €250k in estimated fees. My partnerships pipeline is tracked stage by stage: 41 qualified, 20 active, about ten signed. A quota is the next step, and it's exactly what I'm asking for.

Cut-off 4I ran 105 km in 16:53 in October 2026, so I'm fine with repetition. Six months of executive search meant five interviews a day and far more calls that went nowhere. You hear no, you dial the next number.

Cut-off 5I build things because they help me sell. Every tool on this page started with a sales problem: which account, which person, when to call. And when a prospect brings up agents or MCP, I don't have to bluff, because I use them every week.
